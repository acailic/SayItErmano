## Summary for the commercial decision

1. The project **can sell GPL copies and support** — GPLv3 §4/§6 allow
   charging; the binding constraints are Corresponding Source at no further
   charge and no added restrictions on granted rights (§10). This matches the
   already-decided [ADR-0005](../adr/ADR-0005-commercial-sku.md) offer shape.
2. One real copyleft wrinkle exists: the **`global-shortcut-portal` wheel is
   AGPL-3.0-only** and is vendored + installed into the Flatpak
   ([details](#the-agpl-finding-global-shortcut-portal)). GPLv3 §13 permits
   the combination, but a paid Flatpak must also ship that wheel's source, or
   the paid artifact should drop the `portal` extra.
3. Model weights are **not bundled** in any shipped artifact — they are
   downloaded at runtime under MIT / Apache-2.0 / CC-BY-4.0 terms, all
   commercially redistributable; CC-BY-4.0 adds an attribution duty if a paid
   build ever bundles Parakeet.
4. Trademarks, not copyright, are the residual risk: provider logos
   (OpenAI/Anthropic/… PNGs in `fluidvoice/assets/providers/`) are third-party
   marks; "FluidVoice" is upstream's name. Both need nominative-use care on a
   store page. **OPEN** items listed at the end.

### 1.4 Trademark exposure inside the code (distinct from copyright)

The provider logos in `fluidvoice/assets/providers/` depict third-party marks
(OpenAI, Anthropic, Google Gemini, xAI, NVIDIA, Groq, OpenRouter, Cohere,
Cerebras, Ollama, LM Studio). GPLv3 grants copyright permission for the files
but explicitly does **not** grant trademark rights; GPL §7(e) even
contemplates declining trademark grants. Displaying a provider's mark to
identify that provider's endpoint is classic nominative use, but a *paid
store page* featuring them is a marketing use. **OPEN:** before a paid
launch, either keep marks strictly inside the app's provider-identification
UI (current behavior — `logos.py` shows them only next to the matching
endpoint), or get counsel's read on store-page usage.

### The AGPL finding: `global-shortcut-portal`

- The wheel vendored at `packaging/wheels/global_shortcut_portal-0.1.0-py3-none-any.whl`
  declares `License-Expression: AGPL-3.0-only` in its METADATA (inspected
  today by unzipping the vendored wheel; upstream
  [codeberg.org/marvin1099/python-global-shortcut-portal](https://codeberg.org/marvin1099/python-global-shortcut-portal),
  PyPI metadata agrees).
- Runtime use: `fluidvoice/portal_hotkey.py` imports it
  ([fluidvoice/portal_hotkey.py:285,293](../../fluidvoice/portal_hotkey.py));
  `fluidvoice/session.py:253` probes availability. It is an optional extra
  on the host ([pyproject.toml:46](../../pyproject.toml)) but is **installed
  unconditionally in the Flatpak**
  ([packaging/io.github.acailic.SayItErmano.yml:92,104](../../packaging/io.github.acailic.SayItErmano.yml)).
- Analysis: GPLv3 §13 explicitly permits combining and conveying a GPL work
  with an AGPLv3 work; the combination is then governed by both licenses,
  including AGPL §13's network-interaction terms
  ([GPLv3 text](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/gpl-3.0.html) §13; also in
  [LICENSE](../../LICENSE)). Practical exposure is small — the app has no
  network listener ([ADR-0001](../adr/ADR-0001-no-tcp.md)), and AGPL §13
  concerns interaction *through a network*, not a user-owned unix socket.
- What it means for a paid artifact:
  1. A paid Flatpak is a GPL+AGPL combined work: Corresponding Source must
     include the source of this AGPL wheel (its codeberg repo at the pinned
     version), and the combination cannot be offered under any
     "or-later"/proprietary relicense for that part.
  2. Simplest clean option: the **paid build drops the `portal` extra**
     (Wayland users fall back to DE shortcuts / evdev / wtype per
     [docs/guides/wayland.md](../guides/wayland.md)) or the deb route is the
     paid artifact — it already excludes the wheel.
  3. Since v0.8.2 the deb is the primary channel and the Flatpak is an
     owner-pending submission runbook
     ([docs/packaging/flathub-submission.md](../packaging/flathub-submission.md)),
     so this is a planning constraint, not an incident.

## 3. Redistribution design options for a paid offer

All options assume the free GitHub/source channel stays (already promised in
[docs/PRICING.md](../PRICING.md) GPL statement and
[ADR-0005](../adr/ADR-0005-commercial-sku.md)).

| # | Option | What it sells | Required by the rights inventory | Blocked / constrained by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Plain GPL sale** — paid official .deb / apt channel build | The tested, signed, convenient build | GPL §4/§6: charge any price, provide Corresponding Source at no further charge (the public repo + release tag satisfies §6d); keep license + notices intact; `Depends:` system packages are separate works (§5 aggregate analysis above) | Nothing in-tree. Recipients may re-host the deb — the offer must stay honest (already ADR-0005 positioning) |
| 2 | **Paid Flatpak** | Same build via Flathub/store | Everything in #1 **plus**, because the Flatpak installs the AGPL wheel: Corresponding Source including the AGPL wheel's source; GPLv3 §13 dual-license terms for the combination | AGPL part cannot be relicensed or dropped from an *already shipped* combination; cleanest is excluding the `portal` extra from the paid artifact; Flathub's own submission policies (see [flathub runbook](../packaging/flathub-submission.md)) |
| 3 | **Support/updates entitlement** (ADR-0005's shape: one-time key, update channel, priority support) | Convenience + defined update channel + priority support; key is advisory and never gates dictation | GPL imposes **no** conditions on services/support; the software shipped with the purchase follows #1; no EULA term may restrict GPL rights in the conveyed copy (§10) — the existing [license keys design](../guides/license.md) already states this | Enforcement is capped at support-entitlement abuse (ADR-0005 "No DRM arms race"); a shared key must not reduce what the software does |
| 4 | **Donor / pay-what-you-want** | Funding, nothing gated | Pure GPL distribution, same as #1 minus any entitlement plumbing | No license constraint; economics out of scope here (plan question 3) |

Cross-cutting requirements for every option:

- **Source availability:** every distributed binary (deb, Flatpak, future
  paid artifact) needs equivalent source at no further charge — GPLv3 §6d.
  The repo's normal release flow already publishes source alongside
  artifacts; keep the paid artifact's SHA traceable to a public tag.
- **No additional restrictions:** store-page terms, EULAs, or checkout
  conditions must not purport to limit redistribution, modification, or
  reuse of the conveyed software (§10); the
  [GPL statement drafted for the store page](../PRICING.md) says exactly
  this.
- **Inherited-part obligations:** prompts and SFX are GPLv3 with upstream
  copyright — they travel in the Corresponding Source with notices intact;
  nothing more is owed upstream (no attribution-only clause exists in GPLv3),
  but removing upstream credits would violate §4 notice-keeping.
- **Trademarks:** keep "FluidVoice" mentions nominative with the
  not-affiliated disclaimer (README/site already do); keep provider marks
  inside the app's provider-identification UI, not on marketing creatives,
  pending counsel (OPEN-4).
- **Model weights:** safe to leave unbundled; if bundled, ship a NOTICE with
  MIT/Apache-2.0 texts and CC-BY-4.0 attribution to NVIDIA.

Not available under these facts: a proprietary fork, per-seat field-of-use
restrictions, or dual licensing of the whole tree — the GPLv3 prompts,
sounds, and (for contributions without a CLA) original code make each
impossible without rights the project does not hold.
