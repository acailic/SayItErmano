## What a sale must cover, in this model

A sale is one license at the list price. The model splits costs into:

- **Per-sale cash costs** — processing fee (charged on every
  transaction; refunds are assumed not to claw the fee back —
  conservative), refund loss (rate × price), paid support minutes ×
  hourly rate, variable distribution (GB × cost — near zero because
  the apt repo rides GitHub Pages, artifacts ride GitHub Releases, and
  the Flatpak rides Flathub's CDN), and a chargeback expectation
  (rate × (price + chargeback fee)).
- **The hurdle** — 24 months of fixed monthly cash costs (artifact
  storage/hosting, support tooling, seller-side tax compliance, and
  maintenance time valued at the maintainer rate) **plus** a funded
  12-month maintenance reserve of the same fixed costs.
- **Stated separately, never inside per-sale economics** — one-off
  development to date (sunk), and unpaid community-support time
  (valued at the same hourly rate, shown only in the "economic
  payback" view, per the plan's requirement that unpaid support time
  count).

Two processor columns run throughout: a merchant-of-record (MoR)
checkout that handles VAT as seller of record (~10% + fixed — the
conservative class) versus a direct card processor (~3% + 30c) that
leaves VAT/OSS filings with the seller (priced as a monthly
compliance line).

## What the arithmetic says (and does not say)

Under these assumptions — and only under them:

- **The 9 EUR price point never covers its per-sale costs** at any
  modeled refund rate: paid support time (€10.00) plus fees exceeds
  the price before any fixed cost is funded. That is a property of
  the 15-minute support assumption as much as of the price.
- **At 19 EUR, cash break-even is 2,794–3,380 sales over 24 months**
  depending on the processor column — above the modeled base volume
  (1,500) and below the high volume (5,000). The window-cash table
  shows the same thing continuously.
- **Support minutes, not processor fees, are the dominant controllable
  cost**: ±25% on support minutes moves base-price break-even by
  4,169 sales; the same perturbation of the processor percentage
  moves it by 396. A support policy (docs, recovery tooling, scoped
  entitlement) moves the model more than the checkout choice.
- **List price is the single largest lever** (±25% swings break-even
  by ~17,000 sales at the low end), which is arithmetic, not a
  recommendation: at €14.25 the margin per sale is €0.75, so
  break-even is 20,380; at €23.75 it is 1,843.
- **Paddle's published rate (5% + $0.50) beats the direct column in
  this model** (break-even 2,794 vs 2,883) because the direct column
  carries the €60/mo seller-side VAT/OSS compliance line. The direct
  column's advantage flips only if that compliance line is
  overstated — an assumption, and the biggest structural uncertainty
  on the fixed side.
- **The full economic payback at 19 EUR is "never"** once unpaid
  community-support time (€6.67/sale) is valued at the same rate and
  sunk development must be recovered: per-sale economic net is
  negative. Under these assumptions the paid SKU funds its own
  maintenance reserve but does not repay the sunk effort at mid
  volumes; only the 39 EUR point does (4,019 sales).

These are consequences of the inputs. Changing any assumption in the
`INPUTS` dict changes them; `--check` re-verifies the arithmetic and
that this note's tables match a fresh render.

## Sensitivity note

The generated sensitivity table holds the answer to "which assumption
moves break-even most": at the base case (19 EUR, MoR, 10% refunds),
it is the **list price** (±25% → 20,380 / 1,843 sales), followed by
**paid support minutes** (2,178 / 7,549). Maintenance hours move the
hurdle side (795 sales); processor percentage and refund rate are
mid-sized (396 each); chargebacks barely register at these rates
(32). If the plan wants break-even below the base volume, the two
levers this model ranks first are price and per-sale support time —
both policy choices, not external costs.

## Method and limits

- Arithmetic only, over the assumption table above; no demand,
  conversion, willingness-to-pay, or market-size claim is made or
  implied. Volumes are brackets, not forecasts.
- The break-even solve is exact (smallest integer N with N × net ≥
  hurdle); `--check` re-derives it by substitution (N clears, N−1
  does not) for every price × processor × refund-rate cell, in both
  the cash and economic views, and cross-checks the closed form
  against a naive accumulation loop.
- The refund model charges the processor fee on refunded transactions
  (conservative; providers differ on the percentage part).
- The direct column's €60/mo compliance line is a placeholder for
  seller-side VAT/OSS accounting; real quotes could move it either
  way, and the direct path also concentrates chargeback and fraud
  liability on the seller, which a flat line underprices.
- Maintainer time is valued at a single flat rate for legibility; no
  actual wage claim is made. Sunk development is historical effort,
  not a cash need of the paid SKU.
- Recheck all inputs before any pricing decision; the decision record
  is [PRICING.md](../PRICING.md), not this note.
