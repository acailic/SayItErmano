# One-time purchase cost model (monetization plan R3)

Date: 2026-11-22. Status: planning arithmetic, not a price decision.
Question (plan R3): does a one-time purchase cover its costs? This note
contains **no demand, market or willingness-to-pay claims** — every
input is a labeled assumption. The only content is the arithmetic of
what those assumptions imply, made reproducible by
[`scripts/pricing_model.py`](../../scripts/pricing_model.py).

Regenerate every table below verbatim with
`scripts/pricing_model.py --markdown`; verify the arithmetic with
`scripts/pricing_model.py --check` (re-derives sums, aggregates and
break-even minimality independently; exit 0 = green). The break-even
solve is pinned by unit tests in
[`tests/test_pricing_model.py`](../../tests/test_pricing_model.py).
Context and constraints: the candidate models and license facts in
[2026-09-11-commercial-model-evidence.md](2026-09-11-commercial-model-evidence.md)
and the funnel thresholds in
[pricing-funnel-decision-rule.md](../pricing-funnel-decision-rule.md)
— this note is the "conservative per-sale economics" line item of that
plan.

## Model shape (what is counted, what is deliberately not)

Per sold license, in EUR:

- **Kept revenue** = price × (1 − refund rate). Refunds return the full
  price; processing fees are charged on every transaction *including*
  refunded ones and are not returned by either route.
- **Payment processing**, both routes side by side: merchant-of-record
  ~10% + €0.50 fixed (provider also handles VAT — that service is
  inside the fee) vs direct card collection ~3% + €0.30 (we remain the
  seller of record).
- **Support**: contact minutes × hourly cost, charged on ALL sales
  (refunded buyers contact support too — an assumption, labeled as
  such).
- **Distribution**: apt repo, GitHub Pages and Flathub sit on free or
  bundled tiers, so per-download bandwidth ≈ €0; release-artifact
  storage is a small fixed monthly line instead.
- **Taxes**: MoR route — handled by the provider (marginal €0, paid via
  the fee). Direct route — seller handles EU-OSS compliance and
  invoicing: a fixed monthly compliance cost plus a small per-sale
  bookkeeping cost.
- **One-off development (EUR 14,000) is SUNK** and stated separately:
  it is excluded from per-sale economics and from the break-even
  target, and shown only as a recovery line. A price that funds the
  reserve but not the dev sink is not a failed price.
- **Maintenance reserve**: break-even = the unit count whose total net
  contribution funds a 12-month reserve of fixed costs (storage +
  domain + a paid maintenance-hours budget; the direct route adds the
  tax-compliance line to fixed costs).

## What the arithmetic says (consequences of the assumptions, not claims)

- At a EUR 9 price, support alone (EUR 3.50/sale under the base
  assumption) consumes 39% of the price; net per sale is EUR 3.20–3.93
  and funding the reserve needs ~900–1,100 sales in 24 months. Whether
  any volume materializes is unknown and not modeled.
- At EUR 19, break-even against the reserve is ~300 units (MoR) / ~288
  (direct) under base assumptions; at EUR 39 it is ~125 / ~122.
- The MoR-vs-direct gap per sale (EUR 1.4–3 at these prices) is smaller
  than the uncertainty in support minutes; the direct route's cheaper
  processing is partly eaten by seller-side tax handling (EUR 0.10/sale
  + EUR 20/month compliance). Choosing a route on price grounds alone
  is not supported by this model.
- **Sensitivity**: the maintenance-hours assumption moves break-even
  most in absolute units (154 ↔ 604) because it scales the funding
  target itself; among per-sale assumptions, support minutes dominate
  (263 ↔ 442), then refund rate (280 ↔ 366); the processing percentage
  barely matters within 8–12% (294 ↔ 314). Reducing uncertainty in
  support load per sale is worth more than negotiating the fee.

## Method and limits

Stdlib-only calculator, offline; inputs live in one dict (`INPUTS`) at
the top of the script. `--check` re-derives every published number
independently (fsum of line items, unit-by-unit aggregation, and
minimality of each break-even N: (N−1)·net < target ≤ N·net). The unit
test pins the solve boundary cases (exact multiple, just-short,
non-positive net). Limits: volumes, refund rates, support minutes,
maintenance hours and the EUR 35/h shadow rate are ASSUMPTIONS with no
measured basis in this repository; tax treatment is a simplification
(VAT passed to the buyer, only compliance costed — real obligations
need the seller-entity review flagged in the evidence note); refund
processing may carry additional fixed fees not modeled. This note makes
no claim that any price point is viable, competitive or profitable.
