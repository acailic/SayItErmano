## 1. What an interview can and cannot calibrate

The cost model is only as good as its inputs. Interviews are the
evidence source for the inputs that come from user behaviour; the rest
are provider facts or owner accounting and are out of scope here.

| Plan theme | Pricing-model input (`INPUTS` key) | Assumed today | What an interview moves |
|---|---|---|---|
| Workflow | `volumes` (definition only), `maintenance.hours_monthly` | 300 / 1,500 / 5,000; 10 h/mo | which workflows, apps and desktops the buyer actually has → what the paid build must keep working. **Not** a volume number. |
| Failed alternatives | `refund_rates`, `support.paid_minutes_per_sale` | 5 / 10 / 20 %; 15 min | the quitting triggers and the help people needed → expectation gaps (refunds) and ticket kinds (minutes) |
| Time saved | `prices` | 9 / 19 / 39 EUR brackets | the perceived advantage over their current option → which bracket is even plausible. The *measured* ratio comes from the user-test script, not from here. |
| Privacy | `prices`, `volumes` (definition only) | — | whether on-machine processing is a buying criterion at all → segment definition and price sensitivity |
| Setup tolerance | `support.paid_minutes_per_sale`, `support.community_minutes_per_sale`, `refund_rates` | 15 min; 10 min; 5 / 10 / 20 % | how long people persist before giving up, and whether they self-help or ask → the two support lines and the refund expectation |
| Purchase history | `prices` | 9 / 19 / 39 EUR brackets | amounts actually paid, one-time vs. recurring, who paid → reference prices. The only theme anchored in behaviour rather than opinion |

**Not interview-calibrated** (do not record them as if they were):
`processors.*`, `chargeback.*`, `distribution.*`,
`tax_compliance_monthly`, `tooling_monthly`, `sunk`, `base_case` —
provider facts and owner accounting — and the funnel thresholds, which
were fixed in advance on purpose and may be recalibrated only in
writing per their own rule.

Note the brackets: the cost model's 9 / 19 / 39 EUR are "task example
brackets, not the approved store prices"; the approved prices are the
$49 / $39 points in [PRICING.md](../PRICING.md). Purchase history is
the input that reconciles the two — any change goes through the
commercial gate, never a silent edit of `INPUTS`.

## 2. Screening — who is worth 20 minutes

Any **one** of these qualifies a participant (record the segment):

| Segment | Qualifies when… | Why this segment |
|---|---|---|
| **S1 — active Linux dictation user** | uses dictation / speech-to-text on Linux at least weekly, for the past month, with **any** tool | the current buyer; workflow, time saved, setup tolerance are lived, not imagined |
| **S2 — lapsed user of a cloud tool** | used a network/cloud dictation tool for a few weeks or more, then stopped or cut back, **and** runs Linux daily (or keeps a non-Linux machine only because the tool has no Linux client) | the failed-alternatives theme in its purest form: they know the last straw |
| **S3 — heavy typist with RSI / pain signals** | types several hours a day and reports wrist / hand / arm discomfort or has changed setup because of it (split keyboard, breaks, voice typing attempts) | the segment for which the time-saved and health-adjacent value is highest; may never have dictated |

**Exclude:** builds or sells a dictation / speech tool (not a buyer;
strong bias); no daily writing; professional transcriptionists (not
the audience, as in the user-test script); anyone who cannot say what
OS they use daily.

**Flag, do not exclude:** friends, family and colleagues who know the
maintainer (courtesy bias — do not count them toward the quorum);
participants who already did the moderated user test or reported an
issue (they have seen the product — their failed-alternatives answers
lean toward the current tool; their setup and support answers stay
valid).

**Screener** (chat or phone, ~5 min; record answers verbatim):

1. Which operating system do you use daily? Which distro, and do you
   know if the session is Wayland or X11?
2. Which dictation or speech-to-text tools have you used in the last
   year, what for, and are you still using each one?
3. On a typical day, roughly how many hours do you type? Any wrist,
   hand or arm discomfort, or have you changed your setup because of
   it? (**yes / no only — never ask for or note a diagnosis**)
4. What do you write most: messages and chat, documents and email, or
   code and prompts?
5. Have you built or sold a speech or dictation tool? Do you know the
   SayItErmano project or its maintainer personally? Have you tried the
   app?

**Quorum:** 12–15 interviews, at least 4 per segment, with the same
spread the user-test script asks for — at least 3 who dictate in more
than one language and at least 2 who rely on dictation for
accessibility (they qualify under S1 or S3). Stop adding interviews in
a segment when two consecutive sessions there produce no new theme
notes. Twelve to fifteen interviews calibrate assumptions; they do not
estimate market size or conversion — the plan says so and so should
every write-up.

The commercial gate wants "at least 10 qualified offer conversations"
with a concrete offer. This kit is **not** that: nothing is offered
here. The offer conversations are a later step (the user-test script's
closing questions are the drafted bridge to them).

## 3. The questions

**Format:** one moderator, one participant, 20 minutes — 2 min intro
and consent, 15 min questions, 3 min close. Core questions are asked
of everyone in this order; the last one is segment-specific. If time
runs short, drop from the bottom of each theme rather than skipping a
theme.

**Interviewer rules (read before every session):**

- Do not name our product, a competitor, a price, "one-time", or
  "local" during the questions. If the participant names ours, say
  "note taken, tell me more about *your* setup" and move on.
- Every question must be askable in full sincerity to someone who will
  never use our product. If a question only makes sense as a setup for
  a pitch, it is a pitch — drop it.
- Ask "what happened next?", not "would you…?". Prefer "what made
  you…" over "why did you…" (the latter sounds like a defence).
- Record numbers, tool names and verbatim phrases exactly; do not
  finish their sentences; let silences sit.
- "I don't know" and "nothing" are complete answers — write them down.

| # | Theme | Question | Calibrates | Listen for / avoid |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Workflow | Walk me through the last time you produced a long piece of text — an email, a document, a prompt. Start from the moment you decided to write it. | `maintenance.hours_monthly` (which apps/desktops the buyer lives in), `volumes` definition | the chain of tools, where the text has to end up, what gets copied where. Avoid naming tools. |
| 2 | Workflow | In a typical day, how much of your writing is typed, dictated, or something else — and how do you decide which? | `volumes` definition (how central dictation is), `maintenance.hours_monthly` | switching triggers: length, hands hurting, jargon, language, who will read it |
| 3 | Failed alternatives | Which tools have you tried for getting text in faster than typing — or for dictating? Tell me what happened with each. | `refund_rates`, `support.paid_minutes_per_sale` (ticket kinds) | let them list; the moment they quit; what they did when output was wrong. Never list tools for them. |
| 4 | Failed alternatives | Think of one you stopped using. What was the last straw? *(skip if none)* | `refund_rates` (the quitting trigger that a refund represents) | the "it must never do X" list; whether they asked for money back |
| 5 | Failed alternatives | When one of those tools broke or did something you didn't expect, what did you do next? | `support.paid_minutes_per_sale` vs `support.community_minutes_per_sale` (self-help or ask; which channel) | forum, issue tracker, vendor email, colleague, gave up — and how long they waited |
| 6 | Time saved | Compared with typing, how long does it take you to get a finished, send-ready piece of text the way you do it today? | `prices` (perceived advantage — the gate's "measured advantage" is the user-test's number, this is the story behind it) | correction time, "faster but I have to fix it", what "finished" means to them |
| 7 | Time saved | Since you started working this way, what can you do now that you couldn't before — or what have you stopped doing? | `prices` (the job the tool is hired for) | new habits (dictating on walks, longer documents, another language), not features |
| 8 | Privacy | Where does your voice go today when you dictate — do you know, and does it matter to you? *(S3 who never dictated: "If you've used voice typing on a phone, where do you think that audio went?")* | `prices`, `volumes` definition (is on-machine processing a buying criterion) | workplace policy, client confidentiality, "I never thought about it", "I don't care". Never present local as the right answer. |
| 9 | Privacy | Have you ever chosen, or ruled out, a tool because of where your data went? Tell me about it. | `prices` (privacy as a willingness-to-pay driver), `volumes` definition | what tipped it; whether a policy or a person made the rule; "no" is a finding |
| 10 | Setup tolerance | Tell me about setting up the tools you use now — what did it take, and how much of that would you do again for a new tool? | `support.paid_minutes_per_sale`, `refund_rates` (the abandon point of a paid buyer) | hours spent, model downloads, hotkeys, Wayland, "I gave up", who helped |
| 11 | Setup tolerance | After you've installed a tool like this, what do you expect to happen over the following year? | `maintenance.hours_monthly`, update-term wording of the offer | updates, fixes, new features, "leave me alone"; whether "a year" means anything to them |
| 12 | Purchase history | Tell me about the last software you paid for with your own money — what was it, what did it cost, how did you decide? *(never paid: what did you use instead, and why?)* | `prices` (reference prices) | amount, one-time or recurring, employer vs. personal, how they found it, what nearly stopped them |
| 13 | Purchase history | Have you ever paid for a tool and then regretted it, or asked for a refund? What happened? | `refund_rates`, refund-policy expectation | how easy the refund was, whether they got one, whether they told others |
| 14 | Purchase history | Think of a one-time software purchase and a subscription you've had. What did you expect from each afterwards? | `prices` (one-time vs. recurring framing), `maintenance.hours_monthly` | what "paid once" was expected to include; upgrade feelings; do not say "lifetime" |
| 15 | *(S2 only)* | What would have to be true for you to go back to the tool you dropped? | `prices`, offer shape (their conditions, not ours) | record the conditions; do **not** answer "we do that" — write it and stay quiet |

**Close (3 min):** "Anything I should have asked and didn't?" · "Anyone
else you'd suggest I talk to?" (snowball; take names only with the
person's consent to be contacted) · restate the deletion right and the
moderator contact · thank them. Do not pitch, do not send a link
unless they ask for one.

### Handling rules

- Notes live outside git, in
  `~/.local/share/sayit-ermano-research/interviews/<PARTICIPANT-ID>-<DATE>.md`;
  the consent log in the sibling `consent/` folder. **Never commit a
  note, a consent confirmation, or a contact detail.**
- Recording is a separate opt-in per session; declining it changes
  nothing else in the interview.
- For S3 participants, the note carries only the segment letter —
  no condition, no medical history, no accommodation details beyond
  what the participant volunteers for the workflow story.
- If a participant asks to see the notes, send them their own file
  (access); if they ask to delete, delete the note, the recording and
  the consent row (erasure); either is logged by ID only.
- The app has no telemetry and none is added for this
  ([ADR-0001](../adr/ADR-0001-no-tcp.md),
  [egress audit](2026-11-21-network-egress-audit.md)); interviews and
  voluntarily shared summaries are the sanctioned way to learn, as the
  plan's retention gate already states.

**Rules for changing an input:**

1. An input changes only with **at least 4 interviews** pointing the
   same way and no unexplained contradicting evidence; otherwise
   "defer" with the reason written down.
2. **Stated intent is not purchase evidence.** What someone says they
   would pay is recorded as *stated*; a price change needs a
   *behavioural* anchor from purchase history (an amount they actually
   paid) — the plan's requirement to distinguish intent from purchases.
3. The funnel thresholds are never edited from interview data; if the
   findings bear on listing or store copy, that goes to the funnel
   rule's copy rows, not its numbers.
4. Any `INPUTS` edit is followed by `python3 scripts/pricing_model.py`
   and `python3 scripts/pricing_model.py --check` so the cost-model
   note regenerates in sync (pinned by `tests/test_pricing_model.py`).
5. The decision record is [PRICING.md](../PRICING.md) and the
   commercial gate's offer sheet — never the cost-model note and never
   an interview write-up on its own.

## 6. Channel plan

**Who posts:** the owner. Outreach is an owner action in the plan
("contact users … later execution tasks"); no agent posts, DMs or
emails on the project's behalf. **How to post:** follow each
community's rules first (sidebar, flair, moderator pre-approval for
research posts), disclose the maintainer role in the first line,
promise nothing, and invite people to reply — never harvest contact
details from a public thread ("message me", not "drop your email").
Self-selected respondents skew toward strong opinions; record the
channel on the sheet and read the aggregate with that in mind.

| Channel | Reaches | How to approach | Honest caveats |
|---|---|---|---|
| **r/linux** | S1, S3 — Linux professionals | text post, or a reply in an existing dictation / RSI thread; check the sidebar rules first (the [v0.9.0 announcement checklist](../announcements/2026-11-22-v0.9.0.md) already notes r/linux's self-promotion ratio and maintainer disclosure) | low reply rate per reader; the paid tier is **not live** — say "preparing", never a price |
| **r/selfhosted** | S1–S3 who care about local-first | text post; lead with "research, not release"; keep install commands out of it | skews privacy-maximalist; not representative of the whole audience |
| **Accessibility communities** (screen-reader and motor-impairment groups, RSI / ergonomics boards, distro accessibility lists) | S1 accessibility users, S3 | ask the moderators before any post; offer text, voice or screen-reader-friendly call formats; never DM members | most such communities gate research posts — a moderator-approved post is the only route |
| **Existing GitHub issue reporters / commenters** | S1 who already use the app | reply **in the issue thread** inviting a chat; never DM or email from commit metadata | thin pool today (below); everyone here has seen the product — flag "Seen the app: Y" |
| **Local LUGs** (meetings, mailing lists, Matrix / IRC channels) | S1, S3; multilingual users | ask the organizer for a 10-minute slot or a list post; run interviews on the spot after the meeting | small rooms; for Serbian-speaking groups use the Cyrillic consent script |
